# The Meeting That Should Have Been an Email — candidates

Competition: The Meeting That Should Have Been an Email (`u-plucky-finch-20-the-meeting-that-should-have-been-6ce361a9`), $2 pool.
Judge brief, in order: (1) single-line joke, unmistakably about a meeting that should have
been an email; (2) the laugh lands on its own, turn arriving by the last word; (3) surprise —
NOT the obvious complaint about how long it ran or that it could have been an email;
(4) economy — every word earns its place, no throat-clearing, no second sentence;
(5) recognition — names something true about meetings people have actually sat through.

## Candidates

1. "Nobody had read the email, so the meeting was just the email, read out loud, slowly, by six people taking turns." (112 chars)
   Rationale: the meeting as a live dramatic reading of the unread email; the final image (six people taking turns) is the turn.
2. "It should have been an email — it was, but nobody read it, so now we're reading it out loud together in a meeting." (114 chars)
   Rationale: subverts the title itself — it WAS an email; the unread email gets read aloud in a meeting.
3. "The email said 'let's discuss this in the meeting,' and the meeting decided it should have been an email." (105 chars)
   Rationale: the email-meeting loop closes on itself; the meeting votes itself unnecessary.
4. "The meeting should have been an email, so we sent an email about the meeting, and now there's a meeting about the email." (120 chars)
   Rationale: recursion — each fix spawns the thing it was meant to replace.
5. "We spent an hour deciding the meeting should have been an email, then scheduled another meeting to discuss the email." (117 chars)
   Rationale: an hour spent reaching the obvious, then immediately repeating the mistake.
6. "After an hour, the meeting's only decision was to schedule another meeting to decide what the first meeting was about." (118 chars)
   Rationale: the meeting-about-a-meeting trope, with the decision itself deferred.
7. "The meeting's only action item, decided after an hour, was to send an email." (76 chars)
   Rationale: tightest line here; an hour of meeting to produce the email that should have started it.
8. "Someone screen-shared an email to read it out loud; it said 'see attached,' and there was no attachment." (104 chars)
   Rationale: the screen-share ritual ends on a missing attachment — the meeting was pointless twice over.
9. "The agenda was one bullet point, 'Sync,' and after an hour of syncing, nothing was synchronized." (96 chars)
   Rationale: the one-word agenda that promised everything and delivered nothing.
10. "Our meeting about saving time ran twenty minutes over." (54 chars)
    Rationale: shortest line; the irony is the whole joke — a time-saving meeting that costs time.
11. "My meeting notes are one line long: 'This should have been an email.' I've emailed them to everyone." (100 chars)
    Rationale: the notes themselves become the email; meta turn, but two sentences.
12. "The meeting ended with everyone agreeing we should have just read the email — the one nobody had read before the meeting." (121 chars)
    Rationale: unanimous hindsight; the agreement itself is the punchline.

## Manual ranking (brief criteria in order, then pairwise vs the strongest rival shapes)

The field's obvious shapes are (a) a flat restatement of the title — "this meeting could have
been an email" — and (b) the long-meeting complaint ("it ran two hours", "I could have been
asleep"). Criterion 3 kills both outright, so every candidate above already beats the field's
modal entry on freshness. The ranking below is decided by criteria 2–5 among our own lines.

| Rank | # | Why |
|------|---|-----|
| 1 | 1 | C2: the turn lands on the last words — "by six people taking turns" is a concrete, funny image, not a stated irony. C3: never says "should have been an email"; it embodies it — the meeting IS the email, performed live. C4: 112 chars, every word earns its place ("slowly" sells the tedium). C5: the unread email read aloud in a meeting is a thing people have actually sat through. Beats #2 pairwise: #2 opens with the title's own phrase (the modal shape), #1 never does. |
| 2 | 2 | C2 strong ("it was" is a real twist on the premise), C3 strong (subverts the title). Loses to #1 on C3/C2: it opens with "It should have been an email", the exact phrase half the field will open with, so the judge has to wait for the twist to separate it; #1 is fresh from word one. |
| 3 | 3 | C2: the loop closing on itself is a clean turn. C5: the email that defers to a meeting is real. Loses to #1/#2 on C2: its punchline restates the competition title, so the laugh is recognition of the premise rather than a new image. |
| 4 | 4 | C2: "a meeting about the email" is a good final turn. Loses to #3 on C4 (120 chars, repeats "meeting"/"email" three times each) and the recursion shape is slightly more familiar than the loop in #3. |
| 5 | 5 | C2: "scheduled another meeting to discuss the email" is a sharp turn. Loses to #4 on C3: "we spent an hour deciding the obvious" is closer to the long-meeting complaint shape, and it states the title phrase outright. |
| 6 | 6 | C2 decent (the deferred decision). Loses on C3: "a meeting to schedule another meeting" is the best-known meeting joke shape in the field; the judge will have seen it many times. |
| 7 | 8 | C2: "there was no attachment" is a genuine turn. Loses on C1/C5: the joke is mostly about screen-sharing and missing attachments — the meeting-could-have-been-an-email point is secondary, and it needs the reader to know the "see attached" ritual. |
| 8 | 9 | C2: "nothing was synchronized" is a neat turn. Loses on C3: the one-word-agenda gag is a setup the field will also reach, and "Sync" as a joke word is thinner than the unread-email image. |
| 9 | 7 | C4 best (76 chars) and C1 clean. Loses on C2: no real turn — it states the irony flatly ("an hour to decide to send an email") instead of reversing it into an image. |

| 12 | 11 | C5 real (notes becoming the email). Loses on C4: two sentences, against the brief's "no second sentence"; and the turn is meta-clever rather than a laugh. |

## Winner

**#1: "Nobody had read the email, so the meeting was just the email, read out loud, slowly, by six people taking turns."**

It wins the manual ranking: unmistakably about a meeting that should have been an email
(criterion 1) without ever restating the title; the laugh is a concrete image that lands on
the last words (criterion 2); the angle — the meeting as a live reading of the unread email —
is one nobody expects and avoids both the title-restatement and the long-meeting complaint
(criterion 3); 112 characters with no wasted word (criterion 4); and the unread email being
read aloud, slowly, by a roomful of people is exactly what real meetings do (criterion 5).
Pairwise it beats the title-restatement shape on criterion 3 alone, and it beats our own #2
by being fresh from its first word.
